About the club

The English Club is a school club where students practise English together. We speak, listen, read and write in English in a friendly place where mistakes are welcome.

History

The club was founded on Monday, 9 November 2026 by the school’s English teacher. Its first meeting took place the very next day, on Tuesday, 10 November 2026. Its headquarters is at El Abed Abdelkrim High School in Ali Mendjeli, Constantine, Algeria.

Our goal

We want every member to feel more confident with English and to enjoy using it. A language is not only a school subject: it is a door to new books, music, films, friends and ideas from all over the world, and our club tries to open that door a little wider each week.

What we do

In our meetings, members talk, play word games, read and share stories, listen to songs and watch short videos in English. Everyone gets a chance to speak, and nobody is laughed at for a mistake. Mistakes are simply how we learn.

Who can join

Every student who likes English is welcome, whether a beginner or already very fluent. All you need is curiosity and a smile.

Diabetes Blue Month Open Day

An event of the English Club

Overview

The Diabetes Blue Month Open Day was an event held at El Abed Abdelkrim High School, with 23 members of the English Club taking part. An open day is a day when the doors are wide open: students and teachers can walk in, look around, ask questions and learn something new.

Why blue?

Blue is the colour of diabetes awareness. November is known around the world as the month of diabetes awareness, and 14 November is World Diabetes Day, whose symbol is a blue circle. By choosing this theme, the club joined a worldwide message: know more, take care, and help others do the same.

The day

The open day included several activities. Club members did not just watch: they helped to organise the day, welcomed visitors and used English to share ideas and start conversations.

The teachers’ presence was one of the highlights. A remarkable number of teachers came to take part, and their support gave the members a lot of energy and pride.

Why it mattered

This event showed that a school club can do more than study a language. It can also care about health, bring people together and make a real difference in its school. For many members, it was a chance to use English for a real purpose, which is the best way to practise it.
